Helping Senior Citizens Access Essential Services Safely During COVID-19

Healthcare Social Impact Personal Project Web / Mobile Accessibility

The Senior Citizen App was designed during the COVID-19 pandemic to help elderly people safely access essential services without leaving their homes. The platform connected senior citizens with verified volunteers for grocery delivery, medicines, emergency assistance, doctor consultations, and daily support. The focus was on creating an experience that was simple, accessible, and easy to use for older adults with varying levels of digital literacy.

The Context

Designing a Digital Lifeline for Senior Citizens During COVID-19

The COVID-19 pandemic changed everyday life for everyone, but senior citizens were among the most vulnerable. Simple tasks like buying groceries, medicines, or requesting help suddenly became risky.

To reduce unnecessary travel and provide timely support, the Government of Karnataka introduced the Senior Citizen App , bringing essential services together into a single, accessible mobile experience.

As a UX Designer, my goal was to simplify these interactions and design an interface that felt easy, trustworthy, and comfortable for elderly users with varying levels of digital literacy.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, many senior citizens struggled to access essential services safely.
This raised a simple question:
"How might we help senior citizens access essential services easily and with confidence?"

Understanding Users

Design begins by understanding the people behind the problem.

Senior citizens are not a single type of user. Some are comfortable using smartphones, while others struggle with complex interfaces and unfamiliar interactions.

Research helped uncover challenges beyond technology itself, from remembering medicines and accessing essential services to staying socially connected and getting help during emergencies. Understanding these differences helped shape an experience that supports both confident and hesitant technology users without taking away their independence.

User Persona

Personas helped us understand how different levels of digital confidence shape seniors’ needs, behaviours, and expectations from the SCA experience.

Key Insights

  • 01
    Independence Seniors want support with daily needs without depending on others.
  • 02
    Different Digital Confidence Some seniors use technology comfortably, while others need guidance and reassurance.
  • 03
    Simplicity Builds Confidence Clear labels, readable text, fewer steps, and familiar interactions make technology easier to use.
  • 04
    Connection Matters Social activities, hobbies, and community engagement are as important as daily support.
  • 05
    Help Should Be Immediate Emergency assistance and support must be easy to find and access.

The insights revealed what users struggled with. The 5 Whys helped uncover the root causes behind these challenges.

5 Whys Analysis

Tracing key challenges back to their root causes.

Daily Independence & Connection

Observed Problem

Senior citizens struggle to manage important aspects of daily life independently.

Why 1

They may forget medicines, reminders, events, and other important activities.

Why 2

Daily needs such as medication, activities, social interaction, and emergency support are managed separately.

Why 3

There is no simple way to access these needs from one familiar place.

Why 4

This increases dependency on family members and others for routine support.

Why 5

Existing solutions do not bring together seniors' essential daily, health, safety, and social needs in one accessible experience.

RCA

Senior citizens lack a consolidated and accessible digital experience that supports their health routines, daily activities, safety, and social connection.

Problem Statement

Senior citizens need a simple and consolidated way to manage medicines, reminders, activities, emergency support, and social connections so they can remain active and independent in their daily lives.

This directly supports the original SCA proposition: medicine reminders, social events, hobbies, and emergency alarm support.

Digital Confidence & Accessibility

Observed Problem

Some senior citizens struggle to use digital applications independently.

Why 1

They find many applications confusing.

Why 2

Interfaces often contain small text, unclear icons, and complex navigation.

Why 3

Simple tasks require too many decisions and unfamiliar interactions.

Why 4

Users become afraid of making mistakes and need help from others.

Why 5

Many digital experiences are not designed around age-related accessibility needs and different levels of digital confidence.

RCA

Existing digital experiences often lack the simplicity, accessibility, guidance, and reassurance needed by senior citizens with limited digital confidence.

Problem Statement

Senior citizens with limited digital confidence need an accessible and guided experience with clear labels, readable content, minimal steps, and easy assistance so they can use digital services independently and confidently.

This second RCA is strongly connected to the actual design decisions in SCA: large fonts, high contrast, voice navigation, simplified flows, clear guidance, labeled icons, and one-tap help.

Defining the Experience

Turning user needs into a focused and accessible experience.

Based on the user insights and identified problems, the next step was to define what the experience should prioritize. Features were evaluated around senior citizens’ essential needs, ease of use, accessibility, and urgency during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Feature Prioritization

To keep the experience simple and avoid overwhelming users, features were prioritized based on user need, urgency, and accessibility.

Must Have

  • Emergency Help
  • Medicine Assistance
  • Grocery Assistance
  • Health Support

Should Have

  • Medicine Reminders
  • Volunteer Support
  • Family Communication

Could Have

  • Community Activities

  • Entertainment

Future Scope

  • Advanced Health Monitoring
  • Smart Device Integration
  • Personalized Recommendations

This prioritization helped keep the core experience focused on immediate assistance and independence, while secondary features supported connection and long-term engagement.

Information Architecture

Purpose: To organize essential services into clear, predictable categories that senior users could easily understand and access.

Key Insight: A simple, shallow structure reduced navigation complexity and made important services easier to find.

User Flow

Purpose: To map the steps seniors take to complete key tasks and identify unnecessary complexity or confusion.

Key Insights: Shorter flows with fewer decisions enabled users to complete essential tasks more confidently and independently.

Designing for Accessibility

Making help easier to access, even for users who struggle with technology.

Accessibility became a core part of the design because the app was created for senior citizens, including users with low digital confidence. The goal was not just to make the interface usable, but to make every action feel clear, safe, and easy to complete.

The design focused on reducing confusion through larger touch areas, readable text, simple labels, minimal steps, voice guidance, and quick access to emergency help. Every decision was made to support seniors in completing important tasks independently during COVID.

Accessibility Principles Used

01

Readable Text

Larger font sizes and clear spacing helped seniors read information comfortably.

02

Bigger Touch Targets

Large buttons made tapping easier and reduced accidental mistakes.

03

Clear Labels

Icons were supported with text labels to avoid confusion.

04

Simple Navigation

Important services like medicines, groceries, and emergency help were kept easy to find.

05

Strong Visual Hierarchy

Key actions were highlighted clearly through size, spacing, and placement.

06

Voice Guidance

Voice support helped users who struggled with reading or typing.

07

One-Tap Help

Emergency help was made quickly accessible when users needed immediate support.

Bringing the Experience Together

From simple flows to a reassuring senior-friendly experience.

After defining the flows and accessibility principles, I brought the experience together through wireframes, final screens, and device mockups. The goal was to make every interaction feel simple, clear, and reassuring for senior citizens. Large text, clear labels, high contrast, friendly visuals, and fewer steps helped create an experience that supported confidence and independence.

User Impact & Business Impact

Designing experiences that create value for both users and the business.

Although this project was conceptual, every design decision was made with clear user and business goals in mind. The focus was on reducing friction, improving accessibility, and creating an experience that could encourage trust, independence, and long-term engagement.

User Impact

The proposed experience was designed to:

  • Help senior citizens complete common tasks with greater confidence.
  • Reduce confusion through clear navigation, larger touch targets, and simplified interactions.
  • Make medicine management and reminders easier to follow.
  • Encourage greater independence with minimal reliance on caregivers.
  • Improve accessibility for users with varying levels of digital literacy.
  • Foster a stronger sense of connection through community and engagement features.
Business Impact

The proposed solution aimed to:

  • Lower the learning curve for first-time users.
  • Encourage higher onboarding completion through a simpler experience.
  • Support long-term engagement with reminders and daily interactions.
  • Increase trust by providing a reliable and accessible platform.
  • Reduce potential support requests by simplifying key user flows.
  • Create opportunities for improved retention through a user-centric experience.

Key Learnings

Every design decision became a lesson in building technology that is simple, inclusive, and human-centered.

Designing the Senior Citizen App reinforced that creating meaningful experiences goes beyond aesthetics. The project highlighted the importance of understanding users, simplifying complexity, and designing with empathy from the very beginning.

01
Accessibility should be built in, not added later.

Large touch targets, readable typography, clear labels, and strong contrast make interfaces usable for everyone.

02
Simplicity reduces cognitive load.

Breaking complex tasks into smaller, guided steps helps users feel more confident and independent.

03
Research drives better decisions.

Personas, user insights, and root-cause analysis ensured that every feature solved a genuine user need.

04
Trust is created through consistency.

Predictable navigation, familiar patterns, and reassuring interactions make users feel comfortable using the product.

05
Designing for seniors benefits all users.

Many accessibility improvements ultimately create a better experience for users of every age.

06
UX is about enabling independence.

The most valuable outcome wasn't adding more features—it was helping users accomplish everyday tasks with confidence and dignity.
